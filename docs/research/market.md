# Market Overview — Personal & Household Life Administration

Status: Phase 0 research. Grounded in live web search, Sept 2026. Claims are cited; anything not cited is our own synthesis, marked as such.

## Category definition

LifeOps sits at the intersection of four existing, separately-monetized categories:

1. **Digital vaults / estate planning** — Trustworthy, Everplans, GoodTrust, Quicken LifeHub
2. **Home inventory / maintenance** — HomeZada, Vorby, the now-defunct Centriq
3. **Family organizers / calendars** — Cozi, OurHome, Nestify, Babs
4. **Point trackers** — subscription trackers (Rocket Money), warranty trackers, vehicle maintenance logs, pet record apps, license/certification trackers

No product currently unifies all four under one AI-native ingestion pipeline. That gap is the thesis.

## Timing signal: this category is being actively built right now

This is not a stagnant space we'd be entering cold:

- **Quicken** (a 40-year-old incumbent) launched **Quicken LifeHub** as a dedicated life-admin vault product in 2025 — evidence a large, profitable player believes this is adjacent to its core business.
- **Trustworthy** has already trademarked the phrase **"The Family Operating System®"** and operates a 4-tier pricing ladder up to a $40/mo concierge plan — evidence of real paid demand at the high end.
- **Lifey** (lifeyai.app) and **Babs** (hellobabs.ai) are early-stage / waitlist-only products in 2026 whose core pitch — "snap a photo or forward an email, AI extracts dates/amounts/parties" — is nearly identical to LifeOps's own "Forward it. Photograph it. Forget about it." This is the closest direct validation of our exact mechanic, from more than one independent team, right now. See [competitors.md](competitors.md).
- **Centriq**, a home-appliance-manual/warranty tracker with 400,000+ homes, **shut down entirely on January 31, 2025**. This cuts two ways: (a) real category demand existed at scale, (b) single-purpose products in this space are fragile businesses and users can lose access to organized life records overnight — a trust argument in favor of data portability, and a live pool of orphaned users evaluating alternatives ([HomeBeacon](https://homebeacon.app/alternatives/centriq-alternative), Dib) right now.

## What "fragmented" looks like in practice

A household today assembling the equivalent of LifeOps would need to run, at minimum: Trustworthy or Everplans (vault/estate), HomeZada or Vorby (home inventory + maintenance), Cozi or OurHome (family calendar), Rocket Money (subscriptions), a standalone warranty tracker, a standalone vehicle-maintenance app, a standalone pet-records app, and a standalone license/certification tracker — eight-plus subscriptions and eight-plus places information can go stale or get lost. Every one of these categories independently supports paying products, which is evidence of willingness-to-pay per-domain; nobody has captured the value of unifying them.

## Unvalidated market-size claim (flagged, do not repeat externally without a primary source)

One 2026 secondary source (household-app roundup) states "75% of people admit their essential information isn't well organized." We could not trace this to a primary study in this research pass. Treat as directionally suggestive only; do not cite in external marketing until sourced.
